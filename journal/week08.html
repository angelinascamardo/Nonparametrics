5.2

Method

The Squared ranks test for variances is used to test for equal variances between groups. The data will consist of two random samples. The random samples of \(X_i\)s and \(Y_j\)s must be converted to their absolute deviation from the mean using these two formulas: \[ U_i = |X_i - \mu_1|, i = 1, ..., n \] \[ V_J = |Y_J - \mu_2|, j = 1, ..., m \]

where \(\mu_1\) and \(\mu_2\) are the means from populations 1 and 2. Ranks 1 to \(n +m\) are assigned to the combined sample of Us and Vs. If tied, assign the average of the ranks to each.

Assumptions

  1. Both samples are random samples from their respective populations
  2. Independence within each sample and mutual independence among the two samples
  3. The measurement scale is at least interval

Parametric equivalent (or proof that there is none)

The parametric equivalent of the squared ranks test for equal variances is the parametric Levene test. I have previously learned the Levene test, and therefore have extensive notes on it already.

Hypotheses

Two-tailed:

  • \(H_0\): X and Y are identically distributed, except for possibly different means
  • \(H_A\): Var(X) \(\neq\) Var(Y)

Left-tailed:

  • \(H_0\): X and Y are identically distributed, except for possibly different means
  • \(H_A\): Var(X) < Var(Y)

Right-tailed:

  • \(H_0\): X and Y are identically distributed, except for possibly different means
  • \(H_A\): Var(X) > Var(Y)

Rejection Region

Two-tailed:

  • Reject \(H_0\) if \(T\) is > or < its \(1-\alpha/2\) quantile

Left-tailed:

  • Reject \(H_0\) if \(T\) is < its \(1-\alpha/2\) quantile

Right-tailed:

  • Reject \(H_0\) if \(T\) is > its \(1-\alpha/2\) quantile

Test Statistic and/or p-value

  • Test Statistic: If there are no values of \(U\) tied with values of \(V\), the sum of the squares of the ranks assigned to population 1 can be used as the test statistic: \[ T = \sum_{i=1}^n [R(U_i)]^2 \] If there are ties, subtract the mean from \(T\) and divide by standard deviation to get: \[ T_1 = \frac{T-n \overline{R^2}}{[\frac{nm}{N(N-1)} \sum_{i=1}^N R_i^4 - \frac{nm}{N-1} (\overline{R^2})^2]} \] where \(N = n+m\), \(\overline{R^2}\) represents the average of the squared ranks of both samples combined: \[ \overline{R^2} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^n [R(U_i)]^2 + \sum_{j=1}^m [R(V_j)]^2 \] and \(\sum R_i^4\) represents the sum of the ranks raised to the 4th power: \[ \sum_{i=1}^N R_i^4 = \sum_{i=1}^n [R(U_i)]^4 + \sum_{j=1}^m [R(V_j)]^4 \]

  • Quantiles of exact null distribution of \(T\) are in Table A9 for \(n\) and \(m \leq 10\). For \(n\) and \(m > 10\), use the following approximation based on standard normal quantiles \(z_p\) given in Table A_1: \[ w_p = \frac{n(N+1)(2N+1)}{6} + z_p \sqrt{\frac{mn(N+1)(2N+1)(8N+11)}{180}} \]

  • The p-value is obtained differently for the different tests:

    • Two-tailed: p-value = 2*(smaller of the one-tailed p values)
    • Left-tailed:

\[ P(Z \leq \frac{T-n(N+1)(2N+1)/6}{\sqrt{mn(N+1)(2N+1)(8N+11)/180}}) \]

  • Right-tailed:

\[ P(Z \geq \frac{T-n(N+1)(2N+1)/6}{\sqrt{mn(N+1)(2N+1)(8N+11)/180}}) \]

Method

The modified Squared ranks test for variances is used when there are three or more samples.

Assumptions

  1. Both samples are random samples from their respective populations
  2. Independence within each sample and mutual independence among the two samples
  3. The measurement scale is at least interval

(same as squared ranks)

Parametric equivalent (or proof that there is none)

The parametric equivalent of the squared ranks test for equal variances is the parametric Levene test. I have previously learned the Levene test, and therefore have extensive notes on it already. (same as squared ranks)

Hypotheses

  • \(H_0\): All \(k\) populations are identical, except for possibly different means
  • \(H_A\): Some of the population variances are not equal to each other

Rejection Region

Reject \(H_0\) if \(\chi_0^2\) is greater than its \(1-\alpha\) quantile from the null distribution.

Test Statistic and/or p-value

  • Test Statistic:

\[ T_2 = \frac{1}{D^2} [\sum_{j=1}^k \frac{S_j^2}{n_j} -N(\overline{S})^2] \] where \(n_j=\) number of observations in sample \(j\), \(N = n_1 + n_2 + ... + n_k\), \(S_j=\) sum of squared ranks in sample \(j\), \(\overline{S} = \frac{1}{N} \sum_{j=1}^k S_j\) is the average of all squared ranks, and

\[ D^2 = \frac{1}{N-1}[\sum_{i=1}^N R_i^4 -N(\overline{S})^2] \] and \(\sum R_i^4\) was defined previously in squared ranks.

  • The chi squared distribution with \(k-1\) degrees of freedom is used as an approximation to the null distribution of \(\chi_0^2\).
  • The p-value is approximately the probability of chi squared random variable with \(k-1\) degrees of freedom exceeding the observed value of \(\chi_0^2\).

Completed Homework Problem

  1. A blood bank kept a record of the rate of heartbeats for several blood donors. Is the variation among the men significantly greater than the variation among the women?
men <- c(58, 76, 82, 74, 79, 65, 74, 86)
women <- c(66, 74, 69, 76, 72, 73, 75, 67, 68)

z = c(men, women)

n <- length(men)
m <- length(women)
N <- n+m

# ranks with average for ties
r <- rank(z, ties.method = "average")

# T = sum of squared ranks for men (first n observations)
R_u <- r[1:n]
T <- sum(R_u^2)

T
[1] 1157.25
p_val <- function(T){
  num <- T - n * ((N+1) * (2*N+1)) / 6
  den <- sqrt( m * n * (N+1) * (2*N+1) * (8*N+11) / 180 )
  z <- num / den
  p_right <- 1 - pnorm(z)
  list(T=T, num=num, den=den, z=z, p_right=p_right)
}

Hypotheses

  • \(H_0\): X and Y are identically distributed, except for possibly different means, where x = men and y = women
  • \(H_A\): Var(X) > Var(Y)

Test Statistic and p-Value

  • \(T = 1157.25\)
  • \(p = 0.04964306\)

Rejection Region

  • Reject \(H_0\) if \(p < \alpha\); \(\alpha=0.05\).

Conclusion/Interpretation

  • Reject \(H_0\).

  • There is sufficient evidence to suggest that the variation among the men significantly greater than the variation among the women.
